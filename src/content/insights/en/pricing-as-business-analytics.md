When setting up pricing in B2B, you need to be aware of two things: Competition, and your ability to be flexible in different client scenarios. Your own profitability with each client. Ultimately, pricing analytics is about distinguishing between profitable and unprofitable clients, and pricing strategy is a tool to address the issues on both sides.

Technically, here is what I am doing:

1. Gather all available data, broken down by clients and segments:
   - Revenue
   - Direct costs of service
   - Volume of support and product requests
   - Any other reasonable estimates that help allocate shared and fixed costs across the customer base

   Your ultimate goal is to have this data available in real time, to run experiments on the go rather than wait for reporting cycles. Fortunately, this is how I generally manage to organize things.

2. Run the cost allocation. Obviously, some clients will be more profitable than others, which is a point to discuss with account teams.

3. Observe the distribution of profitability under the current pricing. Highly likely, the current pricing will systematically create profit peaks, where you might be undercut by competitors too often, and profit lows, which drag down your bottom line.

   Of course, high profitability doesn't necessarily mean that your pricing is too high — maybe you have lower costs and are really competitive here. However, you definitely need to investigate these areas to understand what's actually driving profitability.

4. Design and agree on new pricing strategies. These are experimental by nature. Launch them, closely observe how the system behaves, and introduce corrections as you go.

With this, you remain competitive across segments without trying to maximize profit from every single customer — an approach I've too often seen as counterproductive.
